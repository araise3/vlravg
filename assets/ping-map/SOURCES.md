Before it was added, the five-origin model predicted Frankfurt 20.9, Paris 25.2, London 25.4, Warsaw 31.6, Stockholm 22.0, Istanbul 54.0, Madrid 52.4 and Riyadh 108.4 ms. It is one connection, and the cause is not measured. The parameters were not changed after this check. With six origins, the held-origin validation's interpolation MAE was 9.10 ms, worse than the distance baseline's 8.50 ms. Gothenburg's residuals raise the predictions for Berlin when Berlin is held out. The interpolation's advantage seen with five origins therefore did not hold up.

Saint Petersburg lies about 300 km from Helsinki, the only nearby origin, so this check tests interpolation close to an existing sample rather than extrapolation into Russia. Moscow and the rest of Russia remain unmeasured. With seven origins, the held-origin interpolation MAE is 7.65 ms versus 7.59 ms for the distance baseline: still no demonstrated advantage overall.

All seven observed origins now calibrate the entire map independently for each server. For each sample, its routing offset is `measured ms − great-circle distance to server × 0.016 ms/km`. The server baseline is the median of its seven offsets. At an unmeasured location, the estimate is:

```
weight_i = (1000 km / distance to sample_i)^2
correction = sum(weight_i × (sample offset_i − baseline)) / (1 + sum(weight_i))
ping = max(5, baseline + distance to server × 0.016 + correction)
```

At an observed coordinate, use the screenshot's exact value. The interpolated field approaches that value continuously. The inverse-square weighting spreads each sample's routing differences into nearby areas, instead of only overriding the observed country readouts. The denominator's prior weight of 1 fades corrections toward the median baseline away from samples. The 1000 km influence scale, distance slope and 5 ms floor are modeling assumptions, not validated routing measurements. There are no hard city or country boundaries. A roughly 200,000 km/s fiber speed gives 0.01 ms/km round trip; 0.016 allows an assumed 1.6 route-length factor. The distance term is only the extrapolation prior: the combined field is not radial or linear in server distance.

Contours use this same unrounded ping field at 10 ms thresholds from 10 through 150 ms, computed with D3 marching squares (https://d3js.org/d3-contour/contour). A 317 × 244 grid at 6 projected map units covers the map's pan extent. Every polygon boundary is rendered, including disconnected islands and interior holes; artificial closing edges at the grid boundary are omitted. Geometry is cached per server and reused when the country selection changes. Country colors and rounded readouts use the same model. Country selections for Germany, Georgia, Finland, the United Kingdom, Spain, Sweden and Russia preserve the observed cities' reference coordinates and exact screenshot pings.

Seven individual connections cannot establish real routing everywhere or reveal unobserved ISP boundaries. Contour irregularities are interpolations suggested by these samples, not measured coverage boundaries. Display ranges remain a heuristic ±25%, with a minimum ±5 ms; they are not statistical confidence intervals or a claim of validated prediction accuracy. Berlin, Tbilisi, Helsinki, Luton, Jerez, Gothenburg and Saint Petersburg are explicitly labeled observed; other locations are labeled sample-informed estimates. No data from the player's match history is used.

Held-origin prediction validation is reproducible with `node scripts/validate-ping-map.cjs`; the checked-in results and limitations are in [docs/ping-map-validation.md](../../docs/ping-map-validation.md). Each fold removes one city's eight readings and refits all per-server baselines from the remaining cities before predicting. The evaluation compares the production interpolation against distance-only, nearest-city and median-ping baselines without tuning parameters. Tests also verify that changing a hidden city's readings cannot change its predictions.
